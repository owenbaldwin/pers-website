Helping artists find where they fit.

Introduction

Independent artists have more tools than ever. What they're often missing is context: not more data, but a clearer sense of where their work actually fits.

Inceptal started from a problem I understood personally as an independent artist. Musicians are constantly told to find comparable acts, understand their audience, build a brand and find the right promotional channels. In practice, none of that is as straightforward as it sounds.

Most recommendation systems answer a fairly narrow question: what sounds similar? For an artist trying to build a career, that's rarely enough. Two artists can share musical characteristics while having completely different audiences, identities or trajectories. An artist who isn't an obvious sonic match can be a far more useful reference point.

Where does my work actually fit, and what should I do with that information?

The challenge was never a similarity engine. It was a decision.

Creative products are unusually difficult to reduce to clean data. An artist may describe themselves one way while listeners perceive them differently, and their current audience may not represent the one they're trying to reach. There's also an enormous amount of public information out there, but volume and usefulness are not the same thing. That pushed the product away from a conventional database and towards something closer to an evolving research and reasoning system.

In closing

Why this work matters to me

Inceptal has become as much an exercise in product strategy as software development — working across the original user problem, deciding what the product should and shouldn't attempt, the information architecture, the underlying system, and which ideas are worth pursuing.

The biggest lesson has been that AI makes it very easy to build more than you need.

Inceptal is still evolving, so I'm intentionally keeping much of the product itself private for now. But building it has shaped how I now approach AI products more broadly: start with the user's decision, build enough context to understand it, treat generated output with healthy scepticism, and use technology to reduce uncertainty rather than simply produce more information.
